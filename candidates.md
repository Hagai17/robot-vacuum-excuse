# Candidates — The Robot Vacuum's Best Excuse

Gate: one line, 1–140 characters, written to /output/entry.txt.
Judge (tired homeowner), criteria in order: (1) on-topic vacuum excuse for a
missed spot; (2) laugh-out-loud funny; (3) rooted in the vacuum's own world —
sensors, bumpers, dock, map, battery; (4) lands on first read, clean turn at
the end; (5) the vacuum's own deadpan voice.

## Drafts

1. I mapped that corner as 'guest room' in 2023. I do not enter guest rooms uninvited. I am a vacuum, not your mother-in-law. (121)
2. My bumper reported an obstacle. My camera reported a sock. Policy says when sensors disagree, I trust the coward. (112)
3. That spot is under the couch. I have been under the couch. There is a spider the size of a charging dock. You go first. (119)
4. I skipped the rug's dark patch. My cliff sensor filed a report: 'fell off the world, briefly.' I believed it. (110)
5. Battery at 4%. I chose to die in the hallway like a hero instead of finishing the kitchen like a servant. (105)
6. Your dog looked at the spot, then at me. We reached an understanding. The spot is his now. (90)
7. I cleaned that spot yesterday. It is dirty again. At some point this stops being my failure and becomes your lifestyle. (118)
8. The corner is where the Wi-Fi dies. I lost the map, the cloud, and my sense of purpose within four inches. (106)
9. I avoided the Lego. Not out of fear. Out of respect. A machine knows a weapon when it sees one. (95)
10. My dustbin was full of your secrets: three hair ties, a dead battery, and one earring. I stopped to process the betrayal. (118)
11. I have cleaned that spot 214 times. It re-dirties within the hour. My map no longer calls it a floor. It calls it a subscription. (129)

## Duels (judge criteria, both orders considered)

- 6 vs 9: 9 wins — dog diplomacy is cute but generic; the Lego-as-weapon line
  is a machine's own logic (criterion 3/5).
- 3 vs 5: 3 wins — the dock-sized spider is a bigger, more visual laugh.
- 8 vs 4: 4 wins — the cliff sensor misreading a dark rug is a *real* vacuum
  behaviour; the excuse is the machine honestly reporting its own failure.
- 10 vs 1: 1 wins — the guest-room map label is pure vacuum-world logic;
  10's dustbin inventory is funny but drifts toward the owner's world.
- 2 vs 4: 2 wins narrowly — "I trust the coward" is a cleaner end-turn than
  "I believed it", and duelling sensors is as rooted as a cliff sensor.
- 7 vs 2: split. 7 takes criterion 2 (the lifestyle blame-shift is the
  biggest homeowner laugh); 2 takes 3 and 5 (sensors, policy, deadpan).
  Reversed order flips it back — too close to call safely.
- 11 vs 7: 11 wins — it keeps 7's blame-shift laugh but grounds it in the
  vacuum's world: a cleaning count (214) and the map renaming the spot,
  which 7 lacks entirely.
- 11 vs 2 (final): 11 wins. Criterion 1 ties. On 2, "It calls it a
  subscription" lands as hard as "I trust the coward" and needs no policy
  setup. On 3, 11's map + logged clean count is as machine-rooted as 2's
  sensors. On 4, 11's turn is the final word itself. On 5, both are deadpan;
  11's grievance is the vacuum's own bookkeeping, not a borrowed human joke.

## Winner

> I have cleaned that spot 214 times. It re-dirties within the hour. My map no longer calls it a floor. It calls it a subscription.
